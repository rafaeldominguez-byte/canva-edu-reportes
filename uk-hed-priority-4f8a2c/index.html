UK Higher Ed — priority actions

Where the 64 UK higher-education organisations in scope sit against the Canva for Campus commercial model: who has bought staff licences, whose free student unlock is switched on and empty, which prospects already have more active staff than their licence tier would require, and where a plan awarded against eligibility criteria deserves a second look.

Free usage is organic demand, not a problem to be corrected. Under Canva for Campus the student body costs nothing, so a student on a free plan is an implementation task and never an anomaly.

⚠️ Internal use only · Canva Higher Ed EMEA · 1 August 2026 · aggregated data, no personal information

How Canva for Campus works

The institution buys staff licences on Canva Enterprise (feature bundle ENTC). That is the entire paid part of the agreement. Students cost nothing.

The student population sets the minimum number of staff licences through the tier table opposite. Once that minimum is met, the whole student body is unlocked for free in a separate environment (feature bundle CCST) with Pro-level accounts and no brand restrictions. It is all or nothing: there is no partial unlock and no per-student charge. Volume discounts reach roughly 50% from 1,000 licences, and implementation and a customer success manager are included.

At signature a domain claim pulls every account on the institution's e-mail domains into the tenant. Accounts on personal e-mail stay outside it.

Two things follow, and they undo how this report used to be written. Active users minus seats is not a coverage gap — seats only ever cover staff, and students are free and unlimited. And students on free plans inside a Campus institution are simply students who have not been provisioned yet: an implementation task, never an anomaly.

Canva Education is a separate, free K-12 product. It is not Canva for Campus and it is never counted as Campus coverage in this report.

Live agreements — utilisation, tier gap and renewal

Staff licence utilisation is the only seat ratio that means anything under this model. Close to 100% is a true-up or an expansion conversation; a falling ratio at renewal is a risk. The tier gap is how far the seats bought sit below the minimum staff licence count the institution's student FTE would require for a full Campus agreement — in other words, the size of the conversation. Departmental Teams, Business and Pro agreements appear here because the relationship, the procurement route and the Salesforce owner already exist, but the student unlock cannot fire on those plans.

Method and caveats

Definitions

  • Staff licences bought — the quantity on the institution's active Canva Enterprise subscriptions. Under Canva for Campus this is the whole of what the institution pays for.
  • Staff licences in use — people on the institution's own e-mail domains who are live members of a brand carrying the Enterprise feature bundle.
  • Staff licence utilisation — in use divided by bought. Near 100% is a true-up or expansion conversation. A falling ratio into a renewal is a risk. It is the only seat ratio that means anything, because seats never cover students.
  • Students entitled — the institution's student FTE. Once the licence minimum is met the whole student body is entitled, free, all or nothing.
  • Students provisioned — accounts live in the Campus student environment today.
  • Student unlock activation — provisioned divided by entitled. A Success and implementation measure, never a commercial one: nothing more is owed for any of these students.
  • Users outside the tenant — UK-located accounts on the institution's own domains that belong to no brand in the tenant. These are what a domain claim absorbs at signature. Accounts registered with a personal e-mail address are invisible to a domain claim and are deliberately not counted.
  • Active staff today — people on the institution's domains who declared a teaching or staff role and have been active in the last twelve months. Self-declared, therefore directional.
  • Tier gap — the minimum staff licences the student FTE requires, minus the seats bought today. The size of the expansion conversation, not a shortfall the institution owes.
  • Out-of-pocket spend — what individuals at the institution paid for Canva themselves in the last twelve months, in US dollars. Never an institutional licence.

The score

score = 100 × play weight × intensity × confidence weight

  • Play weight — how much stands between the finding and the value. A 1.00 (paid for already, only implementation remains) · B 0.90 (live relationship, procurement route and owner already exist) · C 0.70 (a new commercial conversation is required) · D 0.50 (policy has to be validated before anything can happen).
  • Intensity, per play. A: the share of the entitled student body not yet provisioned. B: 1.00 where seats are bought and nothing is implemented; otherwise the mean of licence utilisation and the share of the required tier already active as staff, multiplied by a size term (0.70 + 0.30 × student FTE ÷ 25,000, capped) and lifted 15% where Salesforce flags the account At Risk. C: active staff ÷ required staff licences, capped at 1, times the same size term. D: eligibility-based free seats ÷ 250, capped at 1.
  • Confidence weight — 1.00 high · 0.85 medium · 0.60 low.
  • The size term exists so that a full twelve-seat agreement at a 1,000-student college does not outrank a ninety-five-per-cent-full agreement at a university forty times larger. Inside Play C the table is ordered on the raw readiness ratio, because that is the question a seller actually asks.

How the play is chosen

  1. A — Student unlock activation if the Campus student environment is live.
  2. B — Staff licence expansion and renewal risk if there is any live or won agreement: Canva Enterprise, a departmental Teams, Business or Pro plan, or seats bought and not implemented.
  3. C — Campus-ready prospect if there is no agreement and the institution has a student body.
  4. D — Eligibility review overrides C where the eligibility signal is the stronger of the two on a like-for-like basis.

Where the student numbers come from

  • HESA Table 2, full-time equivalent students by provider, for all 53 universities: 2024/25 for 34 of them and 2023/24 for the other 19. hesa.ac.uk is not machine-readable from here, so the tables were read from the data templates that transcribe them verbatim, and cross-checked against each institution's own facts-and-figures page wherever one exists. Every institution and its year, measure, source and confidence is listed in the table above.
  • UK-based enrolments only, which is the HESA definition. Reading, Nottingham and Queen Mary publish larger self-reported totals that include overseas campuses and partner programmes; those are deliberately not used, because a Campus agreement is scoped to the UK student body.
  • The three further-education colleges are not in HESA. Their figures are learner headcount from Ofsted inspection reports or the provider's own material, and they are marked as headcount rather than FTE. The Academy of Contemporary Music is the weakest data point in the set and is marked low confidence; it should be confirmed with the account before the number is used commercially.
  • The eight sector organisations carry no student figure. They are suppliers and consultancies, not providers, and the Campus model does not apply to them.
  • Two institutions with large partner and franchise networks — Canterbury Christ Church and Anglia Ruskin — have HESA totals that include students taught by partner providers. The population reachable through a Campus agreement may be materially smaller than the figure shown.

What this report does not say

  • Free usage is organic demand, not a problem to be corrected. It is the strongest buying signal in the data. Under Canva for Campus students are free anyway, so a student on a free plan inside a customer is an implementation task and nothing more.
  • There is no such thing as a coverage gap here. Active users minus seats was the central number in the previous version of this report and it was wrong: staff licences only ever cover staff, and the student body is unlimited once the unlock fires.
  • Eligibility signals are hypotheses pending validation of the FE and HE rules. The current Canva Education and not-for-profit criteria for UK further and higher education have not been applied to these rows; the C4E/NFP team owns that judgement.
  • A single domain can host education faculties, partner schools and colleges, charitable foundations, students' unions, alumni bodies and staff with a dual role. Eligibility-based seats under a university domain are therefore expected to a degree, and the numbers here are an upper bound.
  • Canva Education is a different product. It is free and it is aimed at K-12. It is shown for context in the eligibility section and is never counted as Campus coverage.
  • Teacher, staff and student labels are self-declared at sign-up. Directional, never proof.
  • Two Salesforce matches remain open and were deliberately kept out of the customer status: University of York International Pathway College, a Kaplan joint venture holding three Enterprise seats that are not counted against the University of York, and University of Greater Manchester, whose Salesforce website field points at manchester.ac.uk. Both need a human call.

Data

Users are counted where the e-mail domain belongs to the institution — root domains plus every sub-domain carrying at least 25 users — and the person is located in the United Kingdom. Active means active anywhere in Canva in the last twelve months. Live bundles and live subscriptions are read at 1 August 2026. August sits outside term time, so monthly active figures run low across the board. Aggregated figures only — no personal data, no user names, no e-mail addresses.
